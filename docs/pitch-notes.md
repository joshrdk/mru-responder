# Pitch notes

For presenting to MRU leadership and faculty.

## Before the meeting

- Fill in the meeting date and contact details on the deck's title and closing slides.
- Replace the placeholder card on the tool-sprawl slide with the apps the unit actually uses, and delete any that don't apply.
- Have the prototype open and ready on a phone or laptop before you start, so there's no fumbling.
- Decide who you're asking for what: the Director of Operations for staffing feedback, the Program Manager for approvals.

## Shape of the pitch (about 15 minutes, leaving time for questions)

1. Who you are and how long you've been in the unit. One sentence on why you built this.
2. The problem, framed as the view from inside the unit — and ask them to correct it.
3. Today versus with the app, walking one row (hours is the strongest).
4. The prototype: hand it to someone and let them tap through.
5. How a call flows, then the alert and map screens.
6. The leadership dashboard — ask what else belongs on it.
7. Privacy and safety, before anyone has to raise a hand.
8. What happens when the network fails.
9. The phased plan, honest about approvals being the bottleneck.
10. The four asks. Then stop and wait.

## Questions to expect

**"What if the push doesn't arrive?"** It's a second layer. Radios stay primary, an SMS follows an unacknowledged push, and the app says plainly when it's offline.

**"Are you tracking students?"** Only while on duty, with the switch on the home screen, and no history after a shift.

**"What about patient records?"** Not in this app, deliberately. Counts and operational notes only.

**"Who maintains it after you graduate?"** Documented code, a younger member trained before you leave, and accounts owned by MRU or UHS rather than by you.

**"Why not use an existing product?"** Active911, Aladtec, When I Work and ESO all exist and may already be paid for. Be ready to say what's different: one app built for a collegiate unit, where certification levels, field-training progression and the 4-hour weekly commitment are first-class concepts rather than workarounds. If the unit already pays for something that does the job, say so honestly.

**"How much of your time will this take?"** 8–10 hours a week, and your MRU shifts come first.

## What makes it land

The strongest parts of the pitch came from inside the unit: the number of separate apps people juggle, post-shift reports going missing, and what happens when the network dies at a full stadium. Lead with those, not with the technology.
